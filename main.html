Understanding the Role of the Presiding Officer

What is a Presiding Officer?

The Presiding Officer (PO) is the leader who runs meetings by recognizing members to speak or move. Also called the chair, this role is modeled after the Speaker of the House of Representatives, or the Vice President or President pro tempore of the Senate.

Main Responsibilities

  • Facilitating debate through recognizing speeches, questions, and motions in a fair and efficient manner
  • Fostering order and trust within the chamber
  • Maintaining parliamentary procedure and ensuring fairness
  • Managing time and keeping the session flowing smoothly

Important Note

POs sacrifice their opportunity to speak in service to colleagues. Effective POs foster order and trust, while weak POs erode a chamber's capacity for meaningful debate.

Key Principles

  1. Fairness: Ensure equal opportunity for all participants
  2. Efficiency: Keep the session moving at an appropriate pace
  3. Accuracy: Apply parliamentary procedure correctly
  4. Neutrality: Remain impartial throughout the session

Getting Elected as PO

Election Process

  1. Nomination: A fellow competitor must first nominate you
  2. Voting: A majority of the chamber must vote for you
  3. Single ballot election is used
  4. Runoff elections may occur if there are more than two candidates

The Presiding Officer Speech

During election, you'll give a brief speech (usually 30 seconds to 1 minute). Include:

  • List your qualifications: tournaments PO'ed, practice rounds
  • State a compelling reason why they should vote for you
  • Highlight key attributes: fair, efficient, accurate, smooth flow
  • Show personality and have fun with it

Pro Tip

Talk to peers beforehand to secure nominations and votes. Remember, Congress is a social event!

Sample PO Speech

"Fellow legislators, I'm running for Presiding Officer because I have experience from [X tournaments], understand parliamentary procedure, and am committed to running a fair and efficient session. I will recognize speakers based on precedence and recency, maintain order, and ensure everyone has equal opportunity to participate. Thank you for your consideration."

Opening the Session

Immediate Steps After Election

  1. Say: "This chamber will come to order"
  2. State your intention to recognize speakers fairly and balanced around the chamber
  3. Explain procedures clearly
  4. Describe your gavel time signals

Gavel Signals

Signal Meaning
1 strong tap Call meeting to order / End questioning
1 tap 1 minute remaining
2 taps 30 seconds remaining
3 taps Time elapsed
Multiple taps Grace period ended (3:10 max)

Important Reminders

  • You will not call for motions at any time
  • Speakers should seek recognition when they wish to move something
  • Be visible when addressing the chamber (stand)
  • Use a calm, controlled, and caring voice

Sample Opening Statement

"This chamber will come to order. I will use my best effort to recognize speakers fairly and balanced around the chamber. I will recognize based on precedence and recency. My gavel signals are: one tap for one minute remaining, two taps for thirty seconds, three taps for time elapsed. I will not call for motions - please seek recognition if you wish to move something."

Managing Debate: Recognizing Speakers

Basic Recognition Rules

Legislators stand if they wish to be recognized to speak.

Speaker Recognition Priority

  1. First: Students who have not spoken during the session
  2. Next: Students who have spoken fewer times (precedence)
  3. Then: Students who spoke earlier (least recently - recency)

Critical Rules

  • Precedence and recency should NOT reset during a session
  • They SHOULD reset when a new session begins with a new PO
  • Authors have priority when introducing their legislation

Authorship Exception

When a speaker has authorship privileges to legislation being introduced, the presiding officer must recognize the author first, regardless of precedence/recency.

Recognition Phrases

Standard Recognition:

"The chair recognizes Senator [Last Name]"
"The chair recognizes Representative [Last Name]"

Remember

The elected presiding officer may not give a speech while presiding, unless tournament rules specifically allow it. PO service for two hours counts as two speeches for precedence/recency tracking.

Managing Debate: Questioning Periods

Questioning Time Limits

Speech Type Questioning Time
Authorship/Sponsorship 2 minutes
First Negative 2 minutes
All Other Speeches 1 minute

Key Questioning Rules

  • Only the presiding officer recognizes legislators who wish to question
  • One question at a time - multiple-part questions are NOT allowed
  • No permission needed to yield - it's implied by the questioning period

Direct Questioning Format

Alternative format used at some tournaments (often elimination rounds):

  • Questioning periods broken into 30-second segments
  • One questioner per segment who may ask multiple questions
  • Keep separate questioning precedence/recency
  • At 30 seconds: double tap gavel and call next questioner
  • No grace period in questioning

Ending Questioning

Ending Phrases:

"The questioning period has concluded, and the speaker may be seated"

Tips for Speakers

  • Answer succinctly and don't avoid questions
  • If you don't know, redirect to what you do know
  • Paraphrase if you don't understand the question
  • Thank the presiding officer when recognized

Managing Debate: Parliamentary Procedure and Motions

Key Principle

The PO should not call for motions but should pause briefly between speeches to recognize those seeking recognition to move.

Recognition for Motions

Recognition Phrases:

"The chair recognizes [Name]"
"State your point/question"

Calling for Second:

"Is there a second?"

Stating Motion:

"It is moved and seconded that..."

Common Parliamentary Motions

Motion Purpose Second Required Vote Required
Previous Question Close debate on current issue Yes 2/3 majority
Lay on the Table Set aside current issue Yes Majority
Recess Dismiss for specific time Yes Majority
Adjourn Dismiss the meeting Yes Majority
Amend Propose specific change 1/3 of members Majority
Point of Order Question procedure No Chair rules
Personal Privilege Address personal matter No Chair rules

Taking Votes

Voice Vote:

"On the motion to [read motion title] those in favor, say aye. (Pause) Those opposed, say no."

Rising Vote:

"Those in favor... will rise/raise your hands. [Count]. Be seated. Those opposed... will rise. [Count]. Be seated."

Announcing Results:

"The motion [passes/fails] by a vote of [count]"
"The motion is adopted/carried" or "The motion is lost"

Important Notes

  • Previous Question protects minority voice - only recommend if debate is one-sided or repetitive
  • Amendments must be presented in writing and be "germane" (uphold original intent)
  • Points of Order/Parliamentary Inquiry: Say "State that point" or "What is your question?"

📊 Speaker & Questioner Tracking Guide

Automated Tracking System

The PO Control Panel provides an integrated timer and tracking system that automatically reorders senators based on precedence and recency rules.

How the System Works

  1. Setup: Enter all senator names at the beginning of the session
  2. Automatic Ordering: The system automatically places the next speaker at the top
  3. Precedence Priority: Senators with fewer speeches appear first
  4. Recency Priority: Among those with equal speeches, least recent speakers appear first
  5. Timer Integration: Timer automatically switches between speech and questioning modes

Key Features

  • Real-time reordering after each speech or question
  • Visual priority indicators showing who should speak next
  • Automatic timer transitions from speech to questioning
  • Session statistics tracking total activity
  • One-click marking for speeches and questions

Usage Tips

  • Always use the PO Control Panel for live session management
  • The senator at the top of the list should be recognized next
  • Mark activities immediately to keep the list accurate
  • Use the timer integration for seamless session flow

Setting the Agenda

Importance

Setting the agenda at the beginning of the session is important. Thoughtful discussion in caucusing helps avoid issues later.

Methods for Setting Agenda

  1. Proposed Agendas: Offer proposed agendas and vote via single-ballot election until one receives majority
  2. Agenda Committee: One representative from each school collaboratively determines order (binding unless rules suspended)
  3. Issue-Based Committees: Several committees based on different issues

Key Considerations

  • Place legislation with authors present in higher/earlier positions
  • Once set, agenda is part of standing rules
  • Changing agenda requires motion to suspend rules (ill-advised)

Agenda Phrases:

"The chair will now open the floor for docket/agenda nominations"
"The next item of business is [legislation title]"

Remember

Beyond laying an item on the table, changing the agenda requires suspending rules, which is generally ill-advised.

Working with Judges and Parliamentarians

Types of Judges

Judge Type Location Role
Parliamentarian Sits with PO Advises, evaluates holistically, ensures fairness
Scorer Back/side of room Evaluates speakers and PO

Parliamentarian's Role

  • Advising the PO when necessary
  • Ensuring fairness and supervising the chamber
  • Recommending whether amendments are germane

PO Evaluation Standards

  1. Speaker Recognition: Fairness, consistency, distribution
  2. Parliamentary Procedure: Command, accuracy, ruling quickly
  3. Delivery/Presence: Fostering order/trust, vocal/physical presence, eloquence

Working with Judges

  • Consult the parliamentarian when necessary for accuracy
  • Be confident and handle mistakes professionally
  • Handle criticism with grace and apologize if necessary
  • Make judges' experience enjoyable and their life easier
  • Adhere to time limits and clarify names for judges

Maintaining Order and Discipline

Managing the Chamber

Calling to Order:

"I call this house to order"

Handling Disruption:

"Excuse me. Rep. [Name] you are out of order. Please do not [describe behavior] while a fellow competitor is speaking. Thank you."

Formal Ruling (Serious Offense):

"The member is out of order and will be seated"

Incorrect Procedure:

"The chair rules that the motion is out of order [or 'not in order'] because..."

Protocol and Respect

  • Legislators refer to PO as: Mr./Madam Chair, Speaker, or President
  • Legislators may not approach the front table without rising to a point of personal privilege
  • Always be respectful of others
  • Motions ensuring fairness cannot be suspended

Key Principle

The PO's role in maintaining order is crucial for productive debate. Handle disruptions firmly but respectfully.
